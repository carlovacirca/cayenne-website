01Why it matters

The first good reply usually wins the booking.

When someone needs a dentist, they rarely ask just one practice. They send two or three messages, then book with whoever answers first, and answers well.

Three of the four came in while Sam was with a patient. Every one still had a reply within the hour, because the first draft took seconds, not a quiet half hour that never comes.

02The three steps

Write it once. Reply in minutes.

  1. 1

    Write your answers once

    10 minutes, once

    List the questions you are asked most, with your real answers: new patients, prices, parking, nervous patients and emergencies.

  2. 2

    Let AI write the first draft

    20 seconds

    Paste the enquiry and your answers into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Ask for a short, warm reply.

  3. 3

    Check it, then send

    1 minute

    Read it once. Fix any detail that is not right. Send it within the hour.

Keep it private. Never paste medical notes, dates of birth or full patient details. A first name and the question is enough.

03Your prompts

Two prompts, ready to paste.

Fill in the first one once and keep it. Use the second every time an enquiry comes in.

Prompt 1 · Keep this oneYour practice notes
You help me reply to patient enquiries for Harbourside Dental, a family dental practice in Brighton.

Here are my answers to the questions we get most:
· New NHS patients: [yes, no or waiting list]
· Private check-up: [price and how long it takes]
· Nervous patients: [what we offer]
· Parking: [where to park]
· Emergencies: [what to do and who to call]

Write like a friendly receptionist: warm, plain and calm. Keep replies under 80 words. Never promise a time I have not given you. Sign off as Sam, Harbourside Dental.
